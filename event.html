Two ways a plateau tends to show up

If you're used to solving problems by working harder, a plateau is frustrating, because more effort usually isn't what's missing.

When it's a joint

Maybe you finished physical therapy and you're mostly fine, except on the stairs, or in your golf swing, or when you reach for something overhead. You've learned to work around it. I'd rather look at how you're moving now and find out why it still bothers you.

When it's your metabolism

Maybe the scale stopped moving no matter what you did. Or you started a GLP-1, lost weight, and now you're wondering how much of it was muscle. Or you couldn't tolerate the medication at all. In every one of those situations, strength is what protects you, and building it is my job. Decisions about the medication itself belong to you and your prescriber.

Schedule Your Assessment

A comprehensive look at how you move, how strong you are, and what your body needs next.

This private, one-on-one assessment is designed to give you a useful baseline, not just a first Pilates workout.

I'll look at your movement quality, strength, mobility, body composition, health habits, and any specific areas that may be limiting you. The goal is to understand where you are now, what deserves attention, and what your next 5–6 weeks should look like.

What to expect

Plan for 80–120 minutes, with most assessments taking about 90 minutes. The exact length depends on your goals, history, and how much additional movement analysis is appropriate.

You will spend much of the appointment moving. I'll begin with a broad assessment and then take a closer look at the areas most relevant to you.

Your session may include:

  • Whole-body movement assessmentI'll look at balance, mobility, control, coordination, alignment, and overall movement patterns.
  • Strength assessmentI'll evaluate functional strength and identify areas where you may be stronger, weaker, or compensating. This helps establish an appropriate starting point and gives both of us something measurable to build from.
  • Pilates-based movement assessmentYou may work on the mat and classical Pilates apparatus so I can observe how your body responds to different positions, loads, and movement demands.
  • Localized movement analysisIf you have a particular concern, I can take a closer look at areas such as the knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, spine, ankle, or other joints relevant to your movement and goals.
  • Body-fat assessmentWhen appropriate, I can include a body-fat estimate as part of your overall baseline and progress tracking.
  • Health, activity, and goal reviewYou and I will discuss what you are doing now, what you want to be able to do, previous injuries or limitations, and other factors that may affect your program.
  • Potential intake-pattern inventoryIf nutrition, energy, body composition, or health habits are part of your goals, I may also review your usual intake patterns with you to identify areas worth exploring through health coaching.

What you leave with

This is not simply an hour of testing followed by a conversation.

You'll leave with a written assessment summarizing the major findings from your session, along with a practical 5–6 week plan outlining the areas to prioritize next.

Your recommendations may include:

  • strength priorities
  • mobility or movement work
  • Pilates exercises or apparatus work
  • areas that should be monitored or reassessed
  • health-coaching priorities
  • suggested frequency and progression for the next several weeks

At the end of those 5–6 weeks, the original assessment can also serve as a baseline for seeing what has changed.
